# The Wanderer's Radio

## Product Concept, Creative Direction & Build Plan

**Working concept:** A warm, analogue, culturally travelled internet
radio for researchers, coders, writers, scientists, engineers,
designers, and anyone who spends long hours thinking and making things.

**Status:** Concept direction established; initial visual mockups and
six starter playlist datasets created.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. The core idea

The project began with the inspiration of Deluxe Saloon: not copying its
Indian-barbershop aesthetic, but learning from its strongest idea ---
**turn music and memory into a place instead of presenting another
playlist interface.**

The Wanderer's Radio expands that principle into a broader world.

It is for someone who may spend the whole day in a lab, at a desk,
reading papers, writing, coding, analysing data, designing, or simply
thinking. The website sits quietly in another tab and provides music
collected across countries, generations, roads, rooms, and memories.

The emotional premise is:

> **We carry music from everywhere we've lived.**

And the functional premise is:

> **Something to leave playing while you work.**

It should not feel like Spotify, a productivity application, a science
website, a developer portfolio, or a retro-tech gimmick.

It should feel like entering a room that belongs to a curious,
well-travelled person.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 2. Who it is for

The original musical taste and travelling experience provide the seed,
but the finished product is not autobiographical.

The audience can include:

-   researchers
-   programmers
-   wet-lab scientists
-   engineers
-   mathematicians
-   physicists
-   writers
-   architects
-   designers
-   students
-   PhD students and postdocs
-   anyone doing long periods of focused work

The website does **not** need to repeatedly announce that it is "for
coders" or "for scientists."

The user should recognize themselves through the environment.

A notebook, a paper, a laptop, books, a coffee cup, travel objects, a
radio and evidence of unfinished work are enough.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 3. What the project is NOT

Several earlier ideas were intentionally rejected.

### No biological/scientific gimmicks

Do not make UMAPs, stem cells, single-cell analysis, DNA helices,
microscopy, pseudotime, or bioinformatics the visual identity.

Those are specific to one scientist. The finished world should belong to
researchers and makers generally.

### No futuristic aesthetic

Avoid:

-   cyberpunk
-   synthwave visual clichés
-   neon grids
-   holograms
-   blue glowing dashboards
-   RGB gaming aesthetics
-   glassmorphism
-   futuristic laboratory interfaces
-   AI-assistant aesthetics

### No forced coder/researcher humour

Avoid turning the website into a collection of jokes such as:

-   Reviewer #2 jokes everywhere
-   `n = 3` jokes
-   fake terminal notifications
-   "it worked yesterday"
-   constant loading jokes
-   programming memes

A tiny Easter egg may eventually be fine, but this is not the product.

### No productivity application

No:

-   Pomodoro timer
-   task manager
-   productivity score
-   streaks
-   AI DJ
-   chat assistant
-   calendar
-   Slack integration
-   focus analytics

The user already has enough software telling them what to do.

### No Spotify clone

No giant grid of albums, recommendation carousels, social feed,
profiles, likes, followers, or algorithmic discovery interface.

The experience should be closer to **a radio in a room**.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 4. The musical philosophy

The music deliberately ignores genre purity.

The spectrum can move through:

-   Mohammed Rafi
-   Kishore Kumar
-   Lata Mangeshkar
-   Mukesh
-   1960s and 1970s Indian film music
-   Kumar Sanu
-   Udit Narayan
-   Alka Yagnik
-   Anu Malik
-   A.R. Rahman
-   1990s Indian music
-   Indian roadside/truck-driver music
-   Indian barbershop music
-   ABBA
-   Boney M.
-   Eurovision
-   Talking Heads
-   Eagles
-   Queen
-   Michael Jackson
-   Guns N' Roses
-   American 1980s
-   European pop
-   Backstreet Boys
-   Lou Bega / Mambo No. 5
-   late-1990s global pop

The apparent chaos is a feature.

This is what happens when someone grows up with one musical culture,
inherits music from older generations, travels, lives in other
countries, encounters new music, and never discards the old music.

The result is not a genre.

It is a **life accumulated through sound**.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 5. Organize by worlds, not genres

The site should not primarily expose categories such as:

-   Bollywood
-   Rock
-   Pop
-   Disco
-   80s
-   90s

Instead, music is grouped into **places, moments and moods**.

The initial six stations are:

### 1. Sunday Morning

**Music:** Rafi, Kishore, Lata, Mukesh and related classics.

**World:** Indian home in the morning. Tea, newspaper, open window,
sunlight, radio.

**Feeling:** unhurried, familiar, inherited music.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### 2. Nai Ki Dukaan

**Music:** Kumar Sanu, Udit Narayan, Alka Yagnik, Anu Malik and 1990s
Hindi film music.

**World:** an Indian neighbourhood barbershop sometime in the 1990s.

**Feeling:** ordinary life, radio/cassette culture, familiar voices
playing in the background.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### 3. Highway Dhaba

**Music:** Indian road music, truck-driver selections, Hindi/Punjabi
road songs, travelling music and selected classics.

**World:** roadside dhaba at night. Chai, tables, distant trucks, warm
lights, long highway.

**Feeling:** movement, distance, fatigue, road, companionship.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### 4. Continental Breakfast

**Music:** ABBA, Boney M., Eurovision and European pop.

**World:** old European apartment, café or breakfast room. Coffee, rain,
records, restrained European interiors.

**Feeling:** playful, continental, slightly ridiculous in the best
possible way, sincere rather than ironic.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### 5. California Radio

**Music:** Eagles, Queen, Michael Jackson, Talking Heads, Guns N' Roses
and other American/Anglophone radio music.

**World:** American road, room, motel, California evening.

**Feeling:** road trips, FM radio, distance and openness.

This should **not** become a neon synthwave caricature of California.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### 6. Wander

**Music:** everything.

Kishore can be followed by ABBA, then A.R. Rahman, Eagles, Kumar Sanu,
Boney M., Michael Jackson, Talking Heads or Backstreet Boys.

**World:** the traveller's desk.

**Feeling:** this is the philosophical center of the entire project.

The radio is allowed to take you somewhere else.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 6. The Wanderer's Desk

The first canonical environment should be the **Wanderer's Desk**.

This establishes the art direction for every other scene.

The desk should feel accumulated rather than designed.

Possible objects:

-   wooden desk
-   physical radio
-   notebooks
-   books
-   pen/pencil
-   coffee or tea
-   maps
-   postcards
-   old photographs
-   cassette tapes
-   travel tickets
-   railway tickets
-   boarding passes
-   transit cards
-   luggage tags
-   foreign coins
-   folded receipts
-   conference badge
-   printed paper
-   laptop
-   old camera
-   record/cassette sleeves
-   small souvenirs
-   desk lamp
-   window

Objects from India, Europe and America should coexist naturally.

Do **not** communicate geography with giant flags.

A railway ticket, transit card, postcard or receipt is more interesting
than `INDIA / DENMARK / USA`.

The desk should feel **lived in, not Pinterest-perfect**.

Things may be:

-   folded
-   scratched
-   stained
-   used as bookmarks
-   stacked imperfectly
-   handwritten
-   partly hidden behind other objects

## 7. Visual language

The broad visual period is an analogue memory spanning roughly the 1960s
through the early 2000s.

It does not need to reproduce one exact year.

### Materials

Use:

-   wood
-   paper
-   metal
-   glass
-   fabric
-   speaker cloth
-   cassette plastic
-   printed photographs
-   books
-   maps
-   ink
-   warm lamps
-   natural sunlight

### Lighting

Prefer:

-   morning sunlight
-   afternoon warmth
-   incandescent desk lamps
-   evening shadows
-   ordinary darkness at night

Avoid gratuitous colored lighting.

### Texture

Subtle:

-   film grain
-   paper ageing
-   worn edges
-   fingerprints
-   faded printing
-   imperfect wood
-   slightly dusty objects

The site should feel tactile.

## 8. Posters and wall objects

Posters should demonstrate curiosity without shouting "I AM
INTELLIGENT."

Good wall material might include:

-   old Indian travel posters
-   railway posters
-   European exhibition graphics
-   concert posters
-   old cinema posters
-   maps
-   astronomical charts
-   historical scientific illustrations
-   typography specimens
-   museum postcards
-   vintage airline/travel graphics
-   photographs

## 9. The radio is the interface

The main application interface should be a physical radio that belongs
inside the environment.

It should not look like a floating SaaS card over an illustration.

Possible controls:

-   play/pause
-   previous/next
-   volume knob
-   tuning knob
-   frequency/station scale
-   song information
-   station name
-   Stay / Wander switch

Example conceptual layout:

``` text

│                 RADIO                    │
│                                          │
│ INDIA            EUROPE          AMERICA │
```

The visual design should be inspired by real analogue radios/stereos
rather than futuristic interface design.

## 10. Tuning as navigation

The tuning knob is one of the key interactions.

Instead of clicking conventional playlist cards, the listener can tune
across the musical world.

Conceptually:

``` text
INDIA                 EUROPE                 AMERICA

|      |      |        |       |       |       |
──────────●─────────────────────────────────────

Nai Ki Dukaan
```

Turn the knob:

``` text
INDIA                 EUROPE                 AMERICA

|      |      |        |       |       |       |
────────────────────●───────────────────────────

Continental Breakfast
```

A good transition might be:

1.  current music fades
2.  short radio static/tuning sound
3.  dial physically moves
4.  scene changes/crossfades
5.  new station appears
6.  new music fades in

The transition should feel tactile and satisfying, not like a React
route suddenly replacing content.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 11. Stay vs Wander

One useful feature survived the earlier brainstorming.

The physical radio can contain a switch:

``` text
STAY  ───────────  WANDER
```

### Stay

Keep the musical neighborhood coherent.

Example:

Kishore → Rafi → Mukesh → Lata

or:

Queen → Eagles → Talking Heads → Michael Jackson

### Wander

Allow large cultural jumps.

Example:

Kishore → ABBA → A.R. Rahman → Eagles → Kumar Sanu → Boney M. → Michael
Jackson

This is not only a playback feature.

It captures the philosophy of the project.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 12. Time of day

The website should respond to the listener's local time.

Unlike Deluxe Saloon, which has a specific Indian-time radio identity,
this radio travels with its listener.

Possible periods:

-   morning
-   afternoon
-   evening
-   night

The same room can change through:

-   window light
-   lamp state
-   shadows
-   sky
-   overall warmth

At night, do not turn the scene into cyberpunk.

It should simply look like a real room at night.

Later this could become a continuous lighting system rather than four
discrete states.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 13. Scenes should be layered

Final artwork should not be one flattened wallpaper if avoidable.

A scene can be separated into layers such as:

``` text
background-wall.webp
window.webp
desk.webp
radio.webp
books.webp
postcards.webp
tickets.webp
coffee.webp
foreground.webp
```

Benefits:

-   subtle parallax
-   changing window light
-   moving curtain
-   tea/coffee steam
-   physical radio interaction
-   day/night variants
-   small scene changes without regenerating the whole environment

Animation must remain restrained.

The goal is not to show off animation.

The room should feel alive while the user forgets the website is doing
anything.

## 17. Music data model

Start with a curated static library rather than a database.

Example:

``` ts
export interface Song {
  id: string
  title: string
  titleNative?: string
  artist: string
  year?: number
  film?: string
  spotifyId?: string
  youtubeId?: string
  stations: string[]
}
```

A song may belong to several stations.

Example:

``` ts
{
  id: "pal-pal-dil-ke-paas",
  title: "Pal Pal Dil Ke Paas",
  titleNative: "पल पल दिल के पास",
  artist: "Kishore Kumar",
  year: 1973,
  film: "Blackmail",
  youtubeId: "...",
  stations: ["sunday-morning", "wander"]
}
```

Initial target:

**roughly 150--250 carefully selected songs.**

Do not dump thousands of tracks into the site.

**Curation is part of the product.**

## 19. Playback and copyright architecture

The website should **not host copyrighted audio files**.

It should use official playback mechanisms from music/video platforms.

Recommended architecture:

``` text
OUR WEBSITE
│
├── our artwork
├── our station names
├── our curation
├── our interface
├── track metadata / provider IDs
│
└── PLAYBACK PROVIDER
      │
      ├── YouTube official embedded/IFrame player
      │
      └── Spotify official embeds / links where appropriate
```

Important principles:

-   do not download songs from Spotify/YouTube
-   do not rip streams
-   do not cache or proxy copyrighted audio
-   do not re-host MP3 files
-   do not remove required provider attribution/branding
-   use official player/embed APIs according to provider terms
-   link back to the provider when required
-   do not assume embedding creates a blanket exemption from all
    copyright/licensing obligations

For the custom physical-radio experience, the **YouTube IFrame Player
API is a strong candidate** because it provides programmatic player
controls and state events while playback remains through the official
embedded player.

Spotify can also provide official playlist/track embeds and outbound
listening paths.

Exact provider integration should be rechecked against current platform
terms during implementation.

## 22. V1 user experience

The V1 only needs to do one thing extremely well:

> **Open website → enter beautiful room → turn on radio → tune somewhere
> → hear great music → leave the tab open.**

Basic flow:

``` text
page opens
    ↓
scene loads
    ↓
radio is visible
    ↓
user presses play
    ↓
official embedded player initializes
    ↓
station selects a track
    ↓
music plays
    ↓
song ends
    ↓
next curated track
    ↓
user may tune to another station
```

This is enough for a real product.

## 23. Build order

### Phase 1 --- Freeze art direction

Create one canonical Wanderer's Desk scene.

Agree on:

-   palette
-   illustration/photo treatment
-   material texture
-   lighting
-   radio design
-   typography
-   object density
-   level of ageing
-   geographic references

Do not code the full application before this is right.

### Phase 2 --- Finalize six stations

For each station define:

-   name
-   one-line description
-   musical boundaries
-   visual environment
-   key physical objects
-   lighting
-   initial playlist

### Phase 3 --- Curate music

Expand the initial starter playlists toward roughly 150--250 total
carefully selected songs.

Resolve each song to approved provider IDs/URLs.

### Phase 4 --- Produce final scene assets

Generate/design each scene individually at full resolution.

Do not rely on crops from one concept board.

Maintain one consistent visual language across all stations.

Produce:

-   desktop 16:9
-   mobile composition
-   optional layered elements
-   day/night variants where necessary

### Phase 5 --- Design radio as separate asset/UI

The radio should not be permanently baked into every scene.

Create:

-   body/chassis
-   display
-   knobs
-   switches
-   station scale
-   states

This allows real interaction.

### Phase 6 --- Build Next.js shell

Implement:

-   scene
-   navigation
-   radio
-   station state
-   local time state
-   responsive layouts

### Phase 7 --- Integrate playback

Connect provider IDs to the official embedded player.

Implement:

-   play/pause
-   next
-   volume
-   station change
-   song-ended handling
-   Stay/Wander behavior
-   playback error fallback

### Phase 8 --- Polish

Add restrained:

-   tuning static
-   scene crossfade
-   dial movement
-   light changes
-   steam/curtain/parallax where appropriate

Then performance-test mobile and desktop.

## 25. Product principles

When deciding whether to add something, use these tests.

### Keep it if:

-   a well-travelled researcher/coder/writer could naturally have it in
    their room
-   it makes the world feel more lived-in
-   it adds cultural memory without explaining itself
-   it improves listening
-   it makes the radio more tactile
-   it encourages leaving the site open rather than constantly
    interacting
-   it feels warm, curious and human

### Remove it if:

-   it exists mainly because "coders will get the joke"
-   it makes the website futuristic
-   it resembles SaaS
-   it resembles Spotify
-   it makes the user manage something
-   it turns research into a visual gimmick
-   it looks like generic AI retro art
-   it requires explanation to seem clever

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 26. The guiding sentence

If the project starts drifting, return to this:

> **A warm, wandering radio built from music collected across places and
> decades, made to sit quietly beside people during long hours of
> work.**

The music can be culturally chaotic.

The visual world should be calm.

The technology should disappear.

The room should feel lived in.

And the radio should simply keep playing.
